The crypto wallets most people will use are ones they never see. Sign up for an app with your email, and a wallet appears behind the scenes. No MetaMask, no seed phrase, no word "crypto" on the screen.

A handful of infrastructure companies build those invisible wallets. In the last eighteen months almost all of them were bought by payments, custody or exchange companies. The leader, Privy, now belongs to Stripe and reports more than 160 million accounts. Here is who owns what, where they are based, and how fast the market is moving.

What Privy is

Privy sells wallets as an API. When a user signs up with email, SMS or a social login, Privy creates a self-custody wallet for them on the spot, with no external wallet app and no seed phrase to manage (SiliconANGLE).

The user owns the wallet, but neither Privy nor the app can move funds on its own. Privy keeps the keys inside secure hardware (trusted execution environments), so only the rightful owner can sign (Privy docs). The same API also creates server-controlled wallets, for apps that need to sign on a user's behalf, such as scheduled payments.

Privy was founded in 2021 in New York by Henri Stern and Asta Li. It raised about $41 million from Sequoia, Paradigm and Ribbit Capital (The Block). Stripe announced the purchase on June 11, 2025, for an undisclosed sum, and Privy runs as an independent product inside Stripe (Decrypt).

The logic of the deal: Stripe already owned Bridge, the stablecoin company it agreed to buy for about $1.1 billion in October 2024. Bridge issues and moves the digital dollars; Privy supplies the wallets that hold them. Patrick Collison said the plan is to connect Privy's wallets to the money-movement capabilities of Stripe and Bridge (FinTech Futures). Customers include Hyperliquid, Farcaster, OpenSea and Pump.fun.

Market share: Privy holds roughly half

By the numbers companies publish, Privy is the largest consumer embedded-wallet provider. Its 160 million accounts are about half of the roughly 280 million reported by Privy, Dynamic, Magic and MetaMask Embedded Wallets combined. That is my own back-of-envelope estimate, and it is soft for three reasons:

  • The figures are accounts ever created, not active users; MetaMask reports monthly active users instead.
  • Coinbase, Turnkey and Circle don't publish counts, and Coinbase in particular could be large.
  • Fireblocks claims 130 million+ wallets overall, but most are institutional, so it isn't comparable.

No independent research firm publishes market-share data for this segment yet.

Growth: roughly doubling every year

From June 2025 to October 2026, Privy went from 75 million to 160 million accounts, about 75–80% a year. Growth did not slow after the acquisition: roughly 45% in the nine months after the deal, and another 45% in the seven months after that. The number of apps on Privy doubled too, from about 1,000 to more than 2,000 (CB Insights).

Can a solo developer use it?

Yes. Privy is self-serve: you sign up on its dashboard and start on a free tier, with no sales call or partnership needed. The free tier includes all core features plus 50,000 signatures and $1 million of transaction volume a month (Privy pricing). Paid usage is priced per signature, as low as $0.001 each, and enterprise contracts add SLAs, SSO and compliance tools.

The competitors work the same way. Turnkey gives 25 free signatures a month, then charges $0.10 each or $99 a month plus $0.05 per signature on its Pro plan (CoinGape). Dynamic, Magic and thirdweb also have free tiers (CostBench).

One naming trap: privy.io (crypto wallets) is unrelated to privy.com, an e-commerce email and pop-up marketing tool.

What it adds up to

  • The fight is now between bundles. Stripe sells payments plus stablecoins plus wallets (Stripe, Bridge, Privy). Fireblocks sells custody plus wallets (Fireblocks, Dynamic). Coinbase sells an exchange plus wallets. For a developer already on Stripe, Privy becomes the default choice (Sacra).
  • The independents are few. Magic and Turnkey are the main ones left. Turnkey bets on security and policy controls, including wallets for AI agents; Magic is pushing toward automated, AI-driven wallets.
  • Where the people are. New York hosts Privy, Dynamic, Turnkey, Fireblocks' US headquarters and Circle. San Francisco has Magic. Coinbase and much of Consensys hire remotely, and Turnkey's engineers are remote-first. Boston keeps Circle's regulated money business and a Fireblocks office.
  • The open question is custody law. Privy and its rivals sell self-custody so that they aren't custodians. Holding 160 million wallets inside a regulated payments company may test that line (Seedtable).
